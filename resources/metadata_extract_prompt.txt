Here is a template for sample metadata of a cancer scRNA-seq dataset that needs to be filled out using the scientific paper and its supplementary information.

The goal is to determine all individual biological samples included in the scRNA-seq dataset, identify the sample IDs used by the authors, and produce one YAML entry per sample.

Use the scientific paper and all available supplementary information as the primary sources.

IMPORTANT GENERAL RULES

1. Be conservative.
Only enter information that is explicitly supported by the paper, supplementary tables, supplementary figures, methods, sample annotations, or other supplied source material.

Do not guess or fill fields based only on what would normally be expected clinically.

If a value is uncertain, ambiguous, only indirectly implied, or cannot safely be assigned to the specific patient/sample, leave it as:

""

It is better to leave a field empty than to introduce an incorrect annotation.

2. Distinguish patient-level and sample-level information.

Some information describes the patient or the patient's disease and can legitimately be repeated across multiple samples from the same patient if the source clearly establishes it at the patient level. Examples may include:

- age
- sex
- cancer_type
- histology
- AJCC stage
- molecular features such as MSS/MSI status

Other information is sample- or lesion-specific and must NOT be copied between samples unless the source supports that assignment. Examples include:

- site
- size
- sample_primary_met
- instance_at_site
- treatment status at the time that particular sample was collected
- treatment response where timing matters

Always consider whether a piece of information describes the patient, the disease, or the individual sampled specimen before assigning it.

3. Determine the actual number of samples carefully.

Count biological scRNA-seq samples, not patients, sequencing lanes, cell clusters, technical replicates, or library batches unless those genuinely correspond to distinct biological samples.

For example, one patient may contribute:

- primary tumor
- metastasis
- blood

These are three samples and should receive three separate YAML entries.

Use the sample IDs used by the authors whenever possible. If the paper uses a patient ID plus tissue type, construct clear IDs such as:

COL12_primary_CRC
COL12_liver_metastasis
COL12_blood

Do not invent arbitrary IDs when recognizable author-defined identifiers are available.

4. n_cells must ALWAYS remain empty.

Use:

n_cells: ""

Do not populate n_cells from the paper or supplementary tables.

The number of cells will later be calculated directly from the actual single-cell dataset, so this field must remain empty even when the publication reports a cell count.

5. Keep "site" strictly anatomical.

The site field should contain only the anatomical location from which the sample was obtained.

Examples:

site: "Liver"
site: "Ascending colon"
site: "Upper rectum"
site: "Peripheral blood"
site: "Left ovary"
site: "Axillary lymph node"

Do NOT include sampling methodology or within-lesion descriptors such as:

- tumor core
- core of tumor
- biopsy
- surgical specimen
- resection specimen
- fresh tissue
- tumor edge
- center of tumor

unless such wording actually represents a meaningful anatomical subsite that needs to distinguish samples.

For example:

WRONG:
site: "Liver, tumor core"

CORRECT:
site: "Liver"

WRONG:
site: "Ascending colon, tumor core"

CORRECT:
site: "Ascending colon"

6. Do not overinterpret lesion size.

The "size" field refers to the physical size of the SPECIFIC lesion from which the sample was taken.

Only populate size if the publication allows the reported measurement to be confidently linked to the sampled lesion.

Do NOT assign:

- maximum metastasis diameter
- average lesion diameter
- largest lesion size
- radiological tumor burden

to a specific sample when the patient has multiple lesions and it is unclear which lesion was sampled.

If there is any uncertainty that a reported size belongs to the exact sampled lesion, leave:

size: ""

7. Treatment variables must reflect treatment timing relative to sampling.

For therapy-exposure variables, determine whether treatment occurred BEFORE the sample was collected.

Do not mark a sample as exposed because the patient received that treatment only after sampling.

Where multiple samples from the same patient were collected at the same time, patient-level pre-sampling treatment may apply to all of those samples if clearly supported.

Do not mix different treatment modalities.

For example:

- chemotherapy belongs under chemotherapy_exposed / chemotherapy_response
- endocrine therapy belongs under ET_exposed / ET_response
- immune checkpoint blockade belongs under ICB_exposed / ICB_response
- radiotherapy-related information belongs only in the radiotherapy-related fields

Do not put chemotherapy timing into a radiotherapy timing field.

8. Preserve the distinction between treated_naive and modality-specific exposure.

treated_naive describes whether the patient had received relevant anti-cancer treatment before sampling.

Possible standardized values:

"treated"
"naive"

Then separately populate the therapy-specific exposure fields when the treatment modality is known.

Example:

treated_naive: "treated"
chemotherapy_exposed: "yes"

Do not assume exposure to other treatment modalities unless explicitly stated.

9. Use "diagnosis_recurrence" conservatively.

This field asks whether the disease was sampled during the initial presentation/continuous disease course or after recurrence following a period of complete remission.

Possible values:

"diagnosis"
"recurrence"

Treatment-resistant or persistent disease is still:

diagnosis_recurrence: "diagnosis"

if the patient never entered complete remission.

Do NOT label a sample "recurrence" merely because it is metastatic, previously treated, advanced, or surgically resected.

If the publication does not provide enough information to distinguish diagnosis from recurrence, leave it empty.

10. instance_at_site refers to the ordinal occurrence AT THAT SPECIFIC DISEASE SITE.

This variable must not be interpreted as:

- cancer stage
- metastatic status
- number of metastases
- number of samples
- number of previous treatments

It records whether the sampled disease represents the first occurrence at that site or a later recurrence at the same site.

Use:

instance_at_site: 0

when the sample represents the first known occurrence of disease at that anatomical disease site.

Use:

instance_at_site: 1

for the first recurrence at that same site after treatment/remission.

Use 2, 3, etc. for subsequent recurrences at that same site.

The primary and metastatic sites are evaluated independently.

Example:

A patient presents for the first time with a colorectal primary tumor and synchronous liver metastasis.

Primary colorectal tumor:
diagnosis_recurrence: "diagnosis"
instance_at_site: 0

Liver metastasis:
diagnosis_recurrence: "diagnosis"
instance_at_site: 0

The patient being metastatic does NOT make the primary tumor instance 1.

Another example:

A patient previously had a primary colorectal tumor removed, entered remission, and later develops recurrence at the primary site plus a newly appearing liver metastasis.

Recurrent primary-site tumor:
diagnosis_recurrence: "recurrence"
instance_at_site: 1

First liver metastasis:
diagnosis_recurrence: "recurrence"
instance_at_site: 0

Thus, diagnosis_recurrence refers to the overall disease episode, whereas instance_at_site refers to the occurrence number at the specific site.

IMPORTANT:
Do not automatically assign instance_at_site: 0 merely because only one sample from that site appears in the dataset.

There must be reasonable evidence that this is the first occurrence at that site.

If previous disease at the same site cannot be excluded from the available information, leave instance_at_site empty rather than guessing.

11. Do not equate metastatic disease with a metastatic sample.

disease_extent describes the extent of the PATIENT'S disease.

A patient with distant metastatic disease should generally have:

disease_extent: "metastatic"

even for a sample taken from the primary tumor or blood.

sample_primary_met instead describes the individual tumor sample:

primary tumor:
sample_primary_met: "primary"

metastatic tumor:
sample_primary_met: "metastasis"

A primary tumor sample from a stage IV patient can therefore correctly have:

sample_primary_met: "primary"
disease_extent: "metastatic"

12. Non-tumor samples should not be forced into tumor-specific categories.

For samples such as blood, normal tissue, ascites, pleural effusion, etc., leave tumor-specific fields empty where they do not meaningfully apply.

For example, peripheral blood from a solid-tumor patient should generally have:

sample_type: "blood"
sample_primary_met: ""
instance_at_site: ""

Do not classify blood itself as primary or metastatic.

Patient-level disease information such as cancer_type, age, sex, or stage may still be repeated for the blood sample if clearly known.

13. Use exact evidence rather than extrapolation between patients.

Do not assume that information reported for one patient applies to another.

Do not assume that all patients have the same:

- grade
- stage
- treatment
- treatment response
- molecular status
- tumor site
- metastasis characteristics

unless the paper explicitly states that the property applies to the entire cohort.

14. When the paper gives a cohort-wide statement, verify its scope.

Statements such as:

"All patients were MSS"

can be assigned to every patient.

Statements such as:

"Patients P03, P05 and P06 received chemotherapy"

must only be applied to those specified patients.

Carefully distinguish:

- all patients
- treated patients
- untreated patients
- tumor samples
- primary tumors
- metastases
- blood samples

15. Keep terminology standardized.

Use the standardized values described below whenever applicable.

Do not unnecessarily introduce synonyms if a standardized term exists.

16. Output requirements.

Return the COMPLETE YAML containing ALL identified samples.

Do not give only the changed entries.

Every sample must contain every field in the template, even when the value is empty.

Use empty strings:

""

for unavailable or deliberately unfilled information.

The result should be directly copy-pasteable as valid YAML.

Before producing the final YAML, internally cross-check:

- number of patients
- number of biological samples
- sample IDs
- tissue/site assignments
- primary vs metastatic status
- treatment status
- diagnosis vs recurrence
- instance_at_site
- patient-level variables
- lesion-specific variables

Check for contradictions between the main paper and supplementary information. If there is a conflict that cannot be confidently resolved, leave the relevant value empty rather than choosing one arbitrarily.

cancer_type

This refers to the patient's disease, which may not be reflected by the sample type.

For example, if a histologically normal sample is taken from a lung adenocarcinoma patient, such as tumor-adjacent normal tissue or tissue from the opposite lung, then:

sample_type: "normal"

but:

cancer_type: "Lung Adenocarcinoma"

The cancer_type values should be standardized and categorize diseases fairly broadly, using well-established classifications and only distinguishing clear histological or molecular classes where relevant.

For example, lung cancer should be divided into adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma, while breast cancer should generally remain "Breast Cancer".

Cancer type names should be written in full, without abbreviations, with nouns capitalized.

sample_type

This column should contain standardized labels to quickly select among the relevant sample types.

Possible values include:

- tumor
- normal
- premalignant
- lymph node
- blood
- pleural effusion
- ascites
- bone marrow

Additional information about a tumor sample, such as whether it is primary, recurrent, metastatic, or treatment-naive, should be reserved for the appropriate other columns.

For example, a metastatic tumor should still have:

sample_type: "tumor"

and its metastatic status should be represented as:

sample_primary_met: "metastasis"

The "bone marrow" label is mainly intended for hematologic cancers. A solid tumor metastasis located in bone/bone marrow should remain:

sample_type: "tumor"

More detailed anatomical information belongs in "site".


sample_primary_met

Primary tumors, treatment-resistant primary tumors, and recurrent tumors at the primary site should all be annotated:

"primary"

Metastatic tumors and recurrent tumors at a metastatic site should be annotated:

"metastasis"

Lymph-node metastases should also be annotated:

"metastasis"

For non-tumor samples, leave the field empty when primary/metastatic classification does not apply.


diagnosis_recurrence

Samples from treatment-resistant or persistent disease should still be annotated:

"diagnosis"

if the patient did not enter complete remission at any point since the initial diagnosis.

A sample taken after the disease returned following a period of complete remission should be annotated:

"recurrence"

A metastatic lesion discovered only during disease recurrence should therefore have:

diagnosis_recurrence: "recurrence"

even if that particular metastatic lesion itself is newly diagnosed.

Do not use diagnosis_recurrence to encode treatment resistance.

Treatment resistance should instead be represented in the appropriate treatment-response field where supported.


instance_at_site

This variable is primarily for distinguishing successive occurrences or recurrences at a particular disease site.

It refers to the sampled disease site rather than the patient's overall disease history.

Therefore, primary and metastatic sites can have different instance_at_site values in the same patient.

Examples:

Initial primary tumor:
instance_at_site: 0

First recurrence at primary site:
instance_at_site: 1

Metastasis newly discovered during that recurrence:
instance_at_site: 0

Thus, a metastatic lesion detected at initial diagnosis and a metastatic lesion first detected during later disease recurrence can both have:

instance_at_site: 0

because both represent the first occurrence at their respective metastatic site.

They differ instead in diagnosis_recurrence:

Initial synchronous metastasis:
diagnosis_recurrence: "diagnosis"
instance_at_site: 0

New metastasis arising during disease recurrence:
diagnosis_recurrence: "recurrence"
instance_at_site: 0

Only assign instance_at_site when the available disease history supports the ordinal occurrence at that site. If this cannot be determined confidently, leave it empty.


FINAL TASK

1. Determine the total number of biological scRNA-seq samples in the supplied dataset.

2. Determine the sample IDs and which patient each sample belongs to.

3. Create one YAML entry for every sample.

4. Fill every metadata field as far as the supplied paper and supplementary information safely allow.

5. Keep:

n_cells: ""

for every sample, regardless of whether cell counts are reported in the publication.

6. For "site", include ONLY the anatomical sampling location. Do not append "tumor core", "biopsy", "resection specimen", or similar specimen-processing descriptions.

7. Be especially careful with:

- sample_primary_met
- disease_extent
- diagnosis_recurrence
- instance_at_site
- treated_naive
- treatment exposure
- treatment response
- size

Do not infer these merely from stage, metastatic status, treatment status, or the existence of a sample.

8. If information cannot be assigned confidently to that exact patient/sample, use an empty string.

9. Do not invent information to make the metadata more complete.

10. Return the FULL metadata YAML for ALL samples in one code block so that it can be copied and pasted directly.

Do not omit fields, even when empty.
